# Year Ahead Client Structure Rebuild Map

This document replaces the softer "does each section earn its place?"
question with a harder product rule:

> No client-facing section survives unless it is either proprietary,
> proven and replicable, or necessary reference scaffolding.

It is written against the live Year Ahead client report, not against
aspirational future architecture.

---

## 1. Guardrails

### Product rule

The client report must not earn its length through:
- generic astrology filler
- score dashboards posing as interpretation
- one small card claiming a full page
- whitespace that implies luxury but reads as deception

The client report **may** earn its length through:
- proprietary archetypal interpretation
- proven repeatable timing structure
- robust monthly and annual navigation
- transparent technical/reference scaffolding

### Page-budget guardrails

Do not allow the rebuild to collapse the client report into a thin
artifact.

- Hard floor: `36 pages`
- Working target: `40-48 pages`
- Soft ceiling: `52 pages`

If weak material is cut, pages must be reclaimed by denser, more
defensible material - not by whitespace inflation.

### Opening-page choreography

These should be treated as explicit layout rules for the client PDF:

- Page 1: `Report Record` plus `Data Integrity`
- Page 2: `Chart Reference` plus wheel
- Page 3: placements / reference table

Do not let later generic pagination rules override this opening sequence.

---

## 2. Current Failure Pattern

The report has grown section count faster than it has grown
section-worthiness.

The clearest current offender is `VI. Forecast Climate`:
- builder output is mostly scoring metadata plus generic fallback prose
- the template gives small payloads large card/page footprints
- the section reads as a dashboard, not a reading
- it dilutes trust because it looks like quantity-padding

Related but smaller concerns:
- `III. Reading This Forecast` risks being generic front-matter advice
- `VIII. Annual Rhythm` risks becoming ornamental if it remains a
  standalone visual without enough interpretive consequence

The rebuild should therefore be ruthless about removing low-value square
footage while preserving total report weight.

## 3. Section Decisions

### I. Report Record

Decision: `KEEP`

Why it survives:
- necessary opening scaffolding
- supports credibility and operator honesty
- can carry real front-matter weight without pretending to be
  interpretation

Rules:
- stays on page 1
- `Data Integrity` stays on page 1
- do not bloat it with decorative whitespace

### II. Chart Reference

Decision: `KEEP`

Why it survives:
- necessary reference scaffolding
- wheel + placements are real usable anchors

Rules:
- wheel belongs on page 2
- placements belong on page 3
- keep reference-led, not editorially padded

### III. Reading This Forecast

Decision: `REMOVE AS A STANDALONE SECTION`

Why it fails:
- too easy to become generic reader-advice filler
- not proprietary
- not substantial enough to claim a full numbered chapter

Replacement:
- fold only the essential timing-usage instructions into front matter
  or a short bridge near Year Orientation
- maximum intended footprint: `0.5-1 page`, not a freestanding chapter

### IV. Year Orientation

Decision: `KEEP AND STRENGTHEN`

Why it survives:
- real annual navigation value
- proven report-level orientation function
- strong candidate to absorb other weak annual-summary material

Should absorb:
- the strongest useful parts of `Annual Rhythm`
- any surviving annual summary cues that are truly explanatory

Should not absorb:
- generic "field weather" language
- dashboard-style filler disguised as annual guidance

Target footprint: `3-4 pages`

### V. Archetypal Frame

Decision: `KEEP AND EXPAND`

Why it survives:
- directly aligned with proprietary product identity
- strongest candidate for honest page reclamation
- can carry denser, more defensible interpretation than Forecast
  Climate ever could

Should absorb:
- replacement material formerly pretending to live in Section VI
- active archetypal pressures, narrative tensions, refracted patterns,
  and other genuinely EO-owned interpretive layers

Target footprint: `4-6 pages`

### VI. Forecast Climate

Decision: `KILL ENTIRELY`

Why it fails:
- not proprietary enough
- not interpretively strong enough
- not materially useful enough
- too much page cost for too little information
- currently functions like appendix-grade scoring metadata dressed as a
  premium narrative section

Replacement policy:
- do **not** rescue it with better styling
- do **not** rescue it with denser generic prose
- do **not** keep it because the engine can score it

Its reclaimed page budget must move into:
- `V. Archetypal Frame`
- `VII. Year Arcs`
- `IX. Monthly Chapters`

### VII. Year Arcs

Decision: `KEEP AND DEEPEN`

Why it survives:
- proven replicable long-cycle inclusion
- one of the clearest places where the report can honestly carry weight
- stronger client value than field dashboards

Should absorb:
- more interpretive consequence
- clearer long-cycle framing
- the most defensible "why this matters" annual through-lines

Target footprint: `6-8 pages`

### VIII. Annual Rhythm

Decision: `COMPRESS OR PARTIALLY MERGE`

Why it is not safe as-is:
- can become a pretty standalone visual with weak interpretive payoff
- may not deserve a full numbered chapter if Orientation and Monthly
  Chapters already do the real explanatory work

Preferred outcome:
- keep the proven sequence/timeline utility
- reduce standalone footprint
- merge summary function into `Year Orientation` and/or month openers if
  that yields denser pages

Target footprint if retained standalone: `1-2 pages`

### IX. Monthly Chapters

Decision: `KEEP AND STRENGTHEN`

Why it survives:
- one of the most usable reader-facing sections
- proven repeatable inclusion
- best place to reclaim pages honestly after killing weak annual filler

Should absorb:
- more real monthly guidance
- clearer chapter-level meaning
- stronger connection between dated events and lived navigation

This is the primary non-proprietary place where the report should earn
its size.

Target footprint: `12-16 pages`

### X. Turning Point Guide

Decision: `KEEP`

Why it survives:
- proven repeatable inclusion
- strong navigational value
- compact, honest reader utility

Target footprint: `4-6 pages`

### XI. Cycle Ledger

Decision: `KEEP`

Why it survives:
- transparent technical scaffold
- defensible appendix-grade material that still earns space because it
  is concrete, auditable, and useful

Rules:
- keep robust
- do not dress it up as narrative
- use it as honesty-supporting weight, not fake luxury filler

Target footprint: `6-8 pages`

### XII. Technical Appendix

Decision: `KEEP, LIGHTLY COMPRESS IF NEEDED`

Why it survives:
- necessary reference scaffolding
- supports trust and method transparency

Rules:
- keep concise
- definitions should be useful, not verbose

Target footprint: `2-4 pages`

### XIII. Year Integration

Decision: `KEEP`

Why it survives:
- legitimate closing function
- emotionally and structurally appropriate endcap

Target footprint: `2-3 pages`

## 4. Replacement Logic

Killing weak sections is not the same as shrinking the product.

The report should reclaim lost pages by expanding only these zones:

1. `V. Archetypal Frame`
   This is where proprietary meaning should take more square footage.

2. `VII. Year Arcs`
   This is where proven long-cycle interpretation should carry more of
   the annual story.

3. `IX. Monthly Chapters`
   This is where the reader gets practical, time-localized value and
   where denser content feels earned rather than padded.

4. `XI. Cycle Ledger`
   This remains honest technical ballast - real reference weight, not
   decorative weight.

The report should **not** reclaim pages through:
- bigger margins
- more page breaks
- padded cards
- generic bridge copy
- restyled but still low-value sections

## 5. Recommended vNext Section Spine

This is the intended client-facing shape after the rebuild:

1. `Report Record`
2. `Chart Reference`
3. `How Timing Works Here` or front-matter bridge only, if needed
4. `Year Orientation`
5. `Archetypal Frame`
6. `Active Archetypal Conditions` or equivalent proprietary replacement
   only if it is genuinely EO-owned and not a renamed Forecast Climate
7. `Year Arcs`
8. `Annual Sequence` / compressed rhythm layer, only if it still earns
   a standalone slot
9. `Monthly Chapters`
10. `Turning Point Guide`
11. `Cycle Ledger`
12. `Technical Appendix`
13. `Year Integration`

Important constraint:

If Section VI survives in numbering, it must return as something
substantively different from Forecast Climate - not the same scoring
logic with improved prose.

---

## 6. Implementation Order

1. Lock opening pagination:
   page 1 / 2 / 3 rules for Report Record, wheel, and placements.

2. Remove `III` as a standalone chapter:
   keep only essential usage guidance.

3. Delete `VI. Forecast Climate` from the client structure:
   do not iterate on it as a presentational rescue.

4. Compress or merge `VIII. Annual Rhythm`:
   keep only what remains useful after `IV` and `IX` are strengthened.

5. Rebuild page budget honestly:
   expand `V`, `VII`, and `IX` before touching typography or spacing.

6. Re-run page-count verification against the guardrails:
   if under `36`, the answer is missing payload in strong sections - not
   more filler, not more whitespace.

---

## 7. Non-Negotiables

- Do not preserve a section because the engine can compute it.
- Do not preserve a section because it sounds report-like.
- Do not preserve a section because removing it exposes a page-count
  weakness elsewhere.
- Do not let fear of a shorter report justify generic filler.
- Do not let page count fall by simply cutting without replacement.

The client report should feel:
- denser
- more proprietary
- more structurally honest
- less apologetic
- less padded

That is the rebuild target.
