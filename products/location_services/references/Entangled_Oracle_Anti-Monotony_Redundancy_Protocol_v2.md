# Entangled Oracle: Anti-Monotony & Redundancy Protocol (V2)

**Applies to:** Year Ahead, Personal Forecast, Soul Ecosystem, Identity Profile, and future report block libraries.
**Scope:** Editorial and content-quality standard only. Does not alter JSON schema, selector pathways, variables, formulas, or rendering logic unless a separate implementation task explicitly authorizes that work.
**Status:** V2. Supersedes *Anti-Monotony & Redundancy Protocol for Block Content Writing* (V1). V1's diagnosis — that Year Ahead's architecture places many blocks in close proximity, making repetition unusually visible — still holds and still motivates everything below. V2 adds three things V1 didn't cover: a standard against *decorative vagueness* (the failure mode of a block that's technically distinct from its neighbors but commits to nothing), an explicit framework for *when broad resonance is the right tool and when narrow specificity is*, and a hard, separately-labeled boundary around *deterministic prediction of objective fact*, kept apart from everything else in this document so it can't get confused with ordinary editorial certainty.

---

## Part One: Preserving Coherence Without Sameness (from V1 — unchanged)

The current Year Ahead architecture can place a monthly snapshot, dated transit cards, convergence windows, stations, ingress material, and EO constellation lenses in close proximity. That makes repetition especially visible: a reader may encounter multiple cards that name the same domain, repeat the same caution, or arrive at the same practical recommendation within one or two pages. The protocol below is designed to preserve coherence while making each selected block earn its place.

### 1. Core Standard: Every Block Must Add a New Kind of Value

A block is not sufficiently distinct merely because it uses different wording. Each block must contribute at least one primary function not already doing the same work in its immediate neighbors:

- **Orient** — establish the larger context or climate.
- **Locate** — identify where a pattern may show up in lived life.
- **Differentiate** — distinguish one tension, opportunity, or condition from another.
- **Complicate** — introduce nuance, trade-offs, or a second truth.
- **Translate** — turn symbolic material into recognizable human conditions.
- **Sequence** — clarify timing, pacing, or developmental order.
- **Support discernment** — offer a grounded question, observation, or decision frame.
- **Integrate** — connect separate events into a larger pattern without simply summarizing them.
- **Reframe** — offer a new angle that changes how the reader can relate to an existing theme.

A block should not repeat a neighboring block's central claim, emotional register, practical caution, *and* closing instruction all at once. If it does, it is redundant even when every sentence is technically well-written.

### 2. The "One New Thing" Rule

Before approving any block, identify its new contribution in one sentence. Use this internal test: *"After reading the previous block, what does this block allow the reader to understand, notice, or do that they could not already understand, notice, or do?"*

If the answer is vague — "it says the same thing more poetically," "it reinforces the theme," "it gives another version of the caution," "it reminds them to be grounded," "it is technically about a different transit" — the block needs revision. Reinforcement is allowed only when the repeated theme is being developed, contrasted, or made more specific.

### 3. Layer Distinction Rules

Every report layer has a different job. A block should not drift into another layer's job unless it provides a genuinely new angle.

| Layer | Job | Should include | Should avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Year Overview** | Establish the forecast's broad climate and central developmental question | the major pattern across the year; the central trade-off, invitation, or pressure; the broad kind of discernment the year may require | listing every affected domain; a full transit interpretation; month-level advice; giving the ending away early |
| **Archetypal Opening / EO Framing** | Explain how the reader's existing constellation, index, or archetypal pattern meets the year | a distinct personal lens; a relationship between natal pattern and forecast climate; language that changes the forecast's meaning rather than restating it | paraphrasing the transit card beneath it; the same caution in more mystical language; a second generic forecast |
| **Monthly Snapshot** | Name the month's particular rhythm before dated events | a concise atmosphere/pacing condition; a recognizable shift from the prior month; selective emphasis | predicting the cards below it; the same "capacity, boundaries, sustainability" message every month; the same opening construction |
| **Transit Card** | Interpret the specific symbolic contact and its likely field of relevance | what's distinctive about the transit planet; what's distinctive about the natal target; what changes because of the aspect type; a concrete tension, opening, or observation | generic planet boilerplate; a universal "watch for overcommitment"; treating every aspect as pressure-and-revision |
| **Ingress** | Mark a longer chapter through a whole-sign domain | the domain's changing conditions; the kind of expansion/restructuring/disruption/deepening/softening involved; material forms the theme may take | a transit paragraph with a house name stapled on; repeating the monthly snapshot; overexplaining every manifestation |
| **Station** | Describe a directional pause, review, turn, or reorientation | what becomes more noticeable because movement changes; outward progress vs. internal revision where relevant; the specific kind of reassessment | "retrograde = slow down" as reflex; "direct = move forward" as reflex; duplicating the transit card for the same planet |
| **Convergence Window** | Explain why several events matter more together than separately | the shared pattern created by multiple currents; why the overlap changes the experience; a synthesis unavailable from any one card | a poetic summary of surrounding cards; "several currents gather" with no actual convergence logic; broad emotional language that fits any period |
| **Closing / Year Integration** | Identify what the reader may carry forward | a revised relationship to the year's central question; recognition of what's changed in capacity, discernment, structure, identity, or direction; an earned ending | restating the Year Overview; repeating the first practical recommendation; the same closing line every report |

### 4. Repetition Categories to Audit

Redundancy is not one thing. Check all five forms:

- **4.1 Semantic repetition** — the same meaning recurs with minor wording changes ("do not confuse activity with progress" / "avoid mistaking motion for growth" / "more is not always better"). May all be valid, but shouldn't stack in one report unless each is attached to a clearly different mechanism.
- **4.2 Structural repetition** — blocks repeatedly follow the same argument shape (pressure becomes visible → old strategy stops working → watch overextension → choose a sustainable adjustment). Should not become the default template for every square, opposition, ingress, station, and convergence.
- **4.3 Domain repetition** — multiple blocks cycle the same life areas (identity, confidence, public presence, embodiment, direction, self-presentation) without deepening what's actually changing in them.
- **4.4 Instruction repetition** — the same action prescribed throughout (set boundaries; slow down; choose what's sustainable; make a practical plan; gather more information; avoid overcommitting; have a direct conversation). Useful tools, not universal answers — each must be specific to its block's actual mechanism.
- **4.5 Register repetition** — every block feels emotionally identical even when content differs (cautionary, therapeutic, solemn, corrective, gently admonishing, "wise" in the same way, relentlessly capacity-focused). A full report needs variation in movement: steadiness, inquiry, permission, friction, recognition, experimentation, repair, enjoyment, privacy, visibility, commitment, and release.

### 5. The Repeated Phrase Control List

This language is not forbidden — it's limited-use. Within a single report, each phrase family should generally appear no more than once unless its recurrence is deliberately structural and clearly differentiated.

High-risk phrase families: *"widens the field of possibility" · "what needs revision" · "overcommitting, overlooking logistics, or mistaking enthusiasm for capacity" · "choose what is sustainable" · "a direct conversation, more accurate boundary, or revised plan" · "what is genuinely workable" · "make room for information" · "the life you are trying to build" · "do not confuse activity with progress" · "let the pattern gather" · "a helpful question" · "the next adjustment you can actually sustain" · "not a verdict" · "pressure can reveal" · "this is where… can become tangible."*

When a familiar phrase is removed, replace it with one of: a more exact situational observation; a concrete relational condition; a timing distinction; a different kind of question; a meaningful contrast; an embodied or environmental detail; a specific decision criterion; a statement of what may be protected, tested, clarified, built, released, or practiced — not merely a synonym.

### 6. Transit Differentiation Matrix

A transit block must be built from the interaction of **transit planet + aspect character + natal target + natal house/domain + current report context.** No single component is enough alone.

*Transit planet — what kind of process is moving:*

| Planet | Primary editorial terrain |
|---|---|
| Jupiter | expansion, access, confidence, increased scale, generosity, excess, opportunity, belief |
| Saturn | structure, limits, obligation, credibility, time, maintenance, consequences, stewardship |
| Uranus | disruption, liberation, unpredictability, experimentation, deviation, reform, awakening |
| Neptune | permeability, imagination, longing, ambiguity, dissolution, attunement, idealization |
| Pluto | power, compulsion, depth, exposure, leverage, grief, regeneration, irreversibility |
| Mars | action, heat, courage, conflict, urgency, desire, stamina, friction |

*Aspect character — what kind of movement is occurring:*

| Aspect | Editorial question |
|---|---|
| Conjunction | What is becoming concentrated, immediate, or newly inseparable? |
| Square | What mismatch is demanding movement, negotiation, or reconfiguration? |
| Opposition | What appears through contrast, another person, competing needs, or projection? |
| Trine | What can move with less resistance, and what still requires participation? |
| Sextile | What opening becomes real only through choice, response, or engagement? |

*Natal target — what function is being contacted (don't just list keywords; identify the specific role the target plays in the current pattern):*

Sun: direction, vitality, identity coherence, authorship, visible purpose. Moon: regulation, belonging, need, habit, emotional weather, attachment. Mercury: interpretation, language, planning, inquiry, agreements, decisions. Venus: values, attraction, reciprocity, pleasure, aesthetics, exchange. Mars: action style, assertion, conflict response, stamina, desire. Saturn: commitments, internal authority, time, responsibility, fear, durability. ASC: approach, presence, first contact, body, immediate self-definition. MC: contribution, vocational direction, public role, accountability, recognition. Vertex: encounters, thresholds, invitations, relational turning points.

A Jupiter square to the Ascendant, a Jupiter square to Pluto, and a Jupiter opposition to Mars should never resolve to the same "growth, pressure, revision, capacity, boundaries" sentence with nouns swapped. Their distinct mechanisms must be visible.

### 7. Monthly Variation Requirements

No two adjacent monthly snapshots may share more than one of: dominant emotional register; main action verb; primary life domain; practical guidance type; opening sentence structure; closing sentence structure.

Approved monthly modes (use different ones across the year): Foundation (stabilize, maintain, establish conditions) · Invitation (respond, explore, make contact) · Sorting (distinguish, prioritize, reduce noise) · Threshold (recognize a transition) · Friction (negotiate, confront a mismatch) · Visibility (present, contribute) · Private work (recover, incubate, process) · Relational (clarify reciprocity, belonging) · Experiment (try, prototype, deviate) · Harvest (assess results, receive evidence) · Release (simplify, complete, disengage) · Integration (connect lessons, consolidate). A month may contain more than one quality, but its snapshot should have one dominant movement.

### 8. Practical Guidance Rotation

Select practical guidance from the actual symbolic mechanism, not a generic wellness vocabulary. Rotate the form of support: Boundary · Pacing · Evidence · Conversation · Structure · Experiment · Embodiment · Discernment · Receiving · Release · Visibility · Protection · Meaning-making. No practical recommendation should appear automatically because a block is "challenging" — friction doesn't always require a boundary, expansion doesn't always require restraint, inner work doesn't always require withdrawal.

### 9. Adjacent-Block Review Protocol

Inspect the two blocks before and after in the likely rendered sequence. Ask: does this repeat their main claim, their most memorable phrase, their practical instruction, their metaphor family, their emotional temperature, the same domain without a new condition, the same kind of ending? Could this block move elsewhere without changing meaning? If so, it's probably too generic.

Adjacent blocks should differ in at least two of: symbolic mechanism; life-domain emphasis; emotional register; time scale; question type; action mode; sentence rhythm; metaphor family; degree of specificity.

### 10. Convergence Integrity Test

A convergence block is only justified when the combination creates a conclusion individual cards cannot. Before writing one, identify the shared domain/question, the minimum two contributing events, the interaction effect, and the distinct practical implication. Internal formula: *Event A creates ___. Event B creates ___. Together, they create ___ that neither would create alone.*

Weak: *"Several currents gather around work, responsibility, and visibility. Choose a sustainable pace."*
Stronger: *"A public opportunity and a structural review arrive together, making visibility inseparable from the systems that will carry it. The question is not whether to step forward, but whether the role, schedule, and support behind that step are clear enough to survive its first real demand."*

### 11. Metaphor and Image Diversity

Don't rely on one image system for a report's entire emotional logic. High-frequency families to limit: pressure, expansion, widening, containers, foundations, thresholds, currents, patterns gathering, space-making, direction, carrying, performance — all valid EO language, but prone to saturation. Rotate in: Weather (conditions, visibility, timing) · Architecture (supports, repair, access) · Music (rhythm, dissonance, pause) · Craft (iteration, calibration) · Ecology (interdependence, succession) · Navigation (orientation, course correction) · Conversation (terms, listening, negotiation) · Theatre (role, rehearsal, audience) · Domestic life (routines, agreements, belonging). A metaphor earns its place only when it clarifies mechanism, not as decoration.

### 12. Sentence-Rhythm Standards

Monotony is often audible before it's semantic. Avoid repeated openings ("This is a…" / "A ___ current…" / "The focus is…" / "Pressure gathers…" / "This influence…" / "Keep an eye on…" / "The year/month favors…"). Vary with: a lived condition, a contrast, a question, a concrete image, a temporal shift, an observed tension, a direct second-person invitation, or a statement of what becomes easier/harder to recognize.

Avoid repeated endings ("choose what is sustainable," "make the next adjustment you can actually sustain," "turn the mirror into something useful"). Endings should sharpen the question, offer a precise next orientation, or leave a clean, earned image.

### 13. The "No Generic Caution" Rule

Cautions must arise from the specific mechanism, not be attached simply because a transit is strong, difficult, long-lasting, or high-intensity.

Weak: *"Be careful not to overcommit."*
Better, Jupiter through the 4th: *"More room at home may be genuinely available, but a move, renovation, family promise, or domestic role can still outpace the privacy and maintenance it requires."*
Better, Saturn through the 10th: *"Credibility may grow through consistency, but taking responsibility for every loose end can quietly turn a meaningful role into an unsustainable one-person system."*

### 14. The "No Generic Healing Arc" Rule

Don't force every block into wound→wisdom, pain→growth, confusion→clarity, or pressure→empowerment. Some blocks should allow ambiguity, ordinary maintenance, pleasure, quiet recognition, temporary uncertainty, strategic incompletion, rest without moral framing, receiving without earning, or a choice that stays unresolved for a while. Not every symbolic contact requires a breakthrough.

## Part Two: What V1 Didn't Cover (new in V2)

V1 diagnosed *sameness across blocks*. It never addressed a block that's perfectly distinct from its neighbors — different metaphor, different register, different opening — and still says nothing, because it never commits to a claim. That's a different defect, and it needs its own standard.

### 15. The "No Decorative Vagueness" Rule

*(Peer rule to §13 and §14 — operates on a single block regardless of its neighbors.)*

A block can fail even when every sentence is gentle, ethical, and beautifully written, if it refuses to make a clear interpretive move. Softness, ambiguity, or lyrical atmosphere can be used to avoid naming the actual pattern — and that's a defect, not a virtue, even though it can look identical to appropriate humility at a glance.

**High-risk signs:**
- Every claim is cushioned with "may," "might," "could," or "invites."
- The paragraph circles a theme without landing anywhere.
- The language sounds wise but doesn't tell the reader what's distinct about *this* configuration.
- The block offers mood instead of mechanism.
- The final sentence retreats into a generic reflection question.
- The prose avoids being wrong by becoming too vague to be useful.

**The fix is not to become absolute. The fix is to become specific.**

| Instead of | Try |
|---|---|
| "This may invite you to consider where a pattern around visibility could be asking for more care." | "Visibility is not the problem here. The strain comes from being seen before the structure behind your role has caught up." |
| "You may be asked to reflect on whether an old form of belonging still supports who you are becoming." | "A familiar form of belonging can start to feel too narrow now, not because it was false, but because it was built around an earlier version of your needs." |
| "This period could bring an opportunity to revisit how you relate to responsibility." | "Responsibility becomes easier to understand when it is separated from over-functioning. The useful question is not what you can carry, but what is actually yours to maintain." |

**Preferred correction pattern:**
1. Name the symbolic mechanism plainly.
2. Identify the lived-life condition it points toward.
3. Make one clear interpretive claim.
4. Preserve openness by avoiding fixed outcomes, not by avoiding meaning.
5. End with a useful orientation, not a decorative fog bank.

**A concrete, checkable rule (in the style of §5's phrase list): hedge once, don't stack.** One softening qualifier per claim is fine and often necessary. "It might possibly suggest there could potentially be some indication" is the actual failure — three hedges stacked on one clause, which reads as an author who doesn't trust their own observation. If a sentence needs more than one hedge to feel safe, the fix is to sharpen the claim, not add a second qualifier.

**Guardrail against overcorrection (read together with §17):** this rule is not permission to swing toward declarative certainty about outcomes. EO is humble about certainty and *not* hesitant about interpretation — those are two different axes, and this section governs only the second one. A strong block should feel like: *"I am not telling you what will happen. I am showing you the shape of the pattern clearly enough that you can recognize it, test it against your life, and decide what deserves your attention."* Be careful with outcomes. Be clear about patterns. Don't let ethical restraint become editorial disappearance, and don't let beauty hide the absence of a claim.

### 16. Resonant vs. Revelatory: Two Legitimate Scopes, Not a Moral Hierarchy

A separate axis from §15. §15 is about *commitment* (hedged vs. landed) — it applies at every scope. This section is about *scope* (how broadly a claim is meant to apply), and scope is a business and craft decision, not an honesty question.

**Two modes, both legitimate, both neutral:**

- **Resonant (Barnum-shaped):** broad, widely-applicable statements that a large number of readers will recognize in themselves. This is completely normal cognition — the same mechanism underneath poetry, good therapy, and any writing that makes someone feel seen — not a psychological vulnerability to exploit carefully or describe with suspicion. It does real business work: it's what makes a Year Overview or a monthly theme feel immediately recognizable, and what drives the "this really gets me" reaction that brings someone back to the app.
- **Revelatory (explicit):** narrow, individuated claims that follow from *this specific chart's* computed placements, and would read as false or generic for most other readers. This is what justifies the depth (and price) of the premium/deep-report tier — the reader is paying for *their* chart, not a mood.

**Neither is more honest than the other, and neither is the default.** The failure mode isn't choosing resonant over revelatory or vice versa — it's using vague, uncommitted language as a way to avoid picking either one. A resonant statement can be fully landed and unhedged ("You feel everything more than people give you credit for"). A revelatory statement can be fully landed and unhedged ("The strain isn't the promotion — it's that the systems around you haven't caught up to it yet"). Both are correct uses of confident prose. What's wrong is a statement so hedged it fails at being either.

**Scope already maps onto §3's layer table — this section just names what was implicit:**

| Layer | Default scope |
|---|---|
| Year Overview | Resonant-leaning by design — it's supposed to feel widely recognizable before the report narrows |
| Archetypal Opening / EO Framing | Bridges resonant → revelatory — the personal lens that narrows the broad climate |
| Monthly Snapshot | Mixed — resonant atmosphere, revelatory selectivity |
| Transit Card | Revelatory-leaning by design — the specific symbolic contact is the whole point |
| Ingress | Revelatory, at the scale of a life domain rather than a single event |
| Convergence Window | Revelatory — its entire justification (§10) is a synthesis no single card produces |
| Closing / Year Integration | Resonant-leaning, mirroring the Overview, but earned by what preceded it rather than generic |

A writer shouldn't try to make a Year Overview as narrowly specific as a Transit Card, or make a Transit Card as broadly comforting as a Year Overview. Match the scope to the layer's job; land the claim regardless of which scope you're in.

**Forward note:** this mapping is currently editorial discipline — a writer has to know and apply the table above. A future block-system architecture pass is expected to make resonance scope a structural property blocks carry (metadata, selector-visible) rather than something held only in a writer's head. When that work starts, this table is the intended starting vocabulary.

### 17. The Determinism Boundary (separate from §15 and §16 — read this one on its own terms)

This is not a certainty-of-tone question. It's a different category of claim entirely, and conflating it with §15's "commit to your interpretive claim" standard is exactly the mistake to avoid.

**The line:** "This transit favors visibility becoming harder to avoid" is an interpretive claim about a pattern — landed, unhedged, and entirely appropriate regardless of how confidently it's phrased. *"You will marry the person you met at 12, on October 17, 2028, and move 3.17 miles away"* is not a more-committed version of the same sentence. It's a different species of statement: a falsifiable claim about objective future fact, presented as guidance rather than as the symbolic material it actually is.

This distinction has nothing to do with confidence of phrasing — a sentence can be maximally declarative and still stay entirely on the correct side of it, because *"the pattern points toward X becoming harder to ignore"* and *"X will happen, on this date, to this named outcome"* are different kinds of claims no matter how firmly either is written.

**Why this matters beyond craft:** treating a report as a definitive, objective source of cosmic guidance is a legal exposure, and past a point, an ethical one — if a reader without solid footing restructures real decisions and stability around a report because it was written to sound like fact rather than symbolic material, that's a duty-of-care failure, not a writing-quality one.

**Where explicit deterministic prediction is allowed to exist:** the Predictive Sandbox lane, deliberately, as contained experimentation — not in Year Ahead, Personal Forecast, Soul Ecosystem, or Identity Profile, regardless of how §15 and §16 resolve for those products.

**Sentinel's actual trigger condition (per the Observatory Team role spec) is this section, not §15.** Sentinel exists to catch "deterministic output being described as certainty, diagnosis, or prophecy" — an objective-fact claim dressed as guidance. It is not a trigger for a confidently-written interpretive sentence, and should never be invoked to walk back a block that correctly followed §15's no-hedging standard. If a review conflates the two, that review is wrong, not the block.

## 18. Editorial Audit Checklist (updated)

**Per block**
- Does it have a clearly named primary function?
- Does it offer at least one new insight not supplied by its likely neighbors?
- Does it use specific transit, target, house, aspect, or index logic?
- Does it avoid generic caution language?
- Does it avoid unearned certainty or deterministic prediction of objective fact? *(§17 — not the same check as the next item)*
- Does it land at least one clear interpretive claim, rather than hedge-stacking into fog? *(§15)*
- Is its resonance scope (broad/resonant vs. narrow/revelatory) appropriate to its layer's job? *(§16)*
- Does it avoid repeating a known high-frequency phrase family?
- Does its practical guidance arise from the actual mechanism?
- Does its opening and ending differ from nearby blocks?
- Could the block only belong to this symbolic configuration, or is it interchangeable?

**Per library**
- Are all major phrase families distributed rather than clustered?
- Are multiple guidance modes represented?
- Are flowing and challenging blocks genuinely different in movement, not only optimism level?
- Are all twelve monthly snapshots distinct in dominant mode?
- Are convergence blocks actual synthesis rather than summary?
- Do EO overlays add a personal lens rather than duplicate standard transit copy?
- Does the library contain enough pleasure, permission, curiosity, and recognition to balance caution and self-management?
- Does any block anywhere read as decorative fog with no landed claim?

**Per rendered report**
- Does any phrase appear twice on the same page?
- Does the reader receive the same advice in multiple consecutive cards?
- Do two adjacent cards use the same metaphor family?
- Do multiple blocks sound like the same sentence template?
- Does every page contain forward movement rather than thematic circling?
- Does any block cross the determinism line — an objective-fact claim rather than an interpretive one? *(release-blocking, not a style note)*

## 19. Revision Decision Framework (from V1 — unchanged)

When a block fails the protocol, choose the smallest editorial action that solves the issue:

- **Keep** — distinct, specific, functionally necessary.
- **Tighten** — good insight, but repeated setup, duplicated cautions, or unnecessary conclusion language.
- **Re-angle** — structurally sound, but occupies the same emotional/conceptual territory as a neighbor.
- **Replace** — generic enough to fit many unrelated configurations.
- **Remove** — no new information, no new emotional movement, no new practical value.

No block should be retained merely because it's elegant in isolation. It must work in the report sequence.

## 20. Writer's Final Test (updated)

Before finalizing a block, answer in plain language:

1. What is actually different about this symbolic situation?
2. What is the reader likely to recognize here that isn't already stated elsewhere?
3. What emotional register does this block bring to the report?
4. What practical mode does it offer — discernment, structure, conversation, experiment, protection, receiving, release, or something else?
5. What exact sentence would become less true if this block were removed?
6. *(new)* What is the one clear claim this block lands — not implies, not gestures at, actually lands?
7. *(new)* Is that claim resonant or revelatory in scope, and does that match this layer's job?

If the answer to #5 is "the report would still say basically the same thing," rewrite or remove it. If the answer to #6 is "it doesn't really land one," that's the same defect from the opposite direction — rewrite it regardless of how well it passed every other check.

---

## Protocol Summary

Entangled Oracle block writing should create coherence without sameness, and clarity without false certainty. A reader should feel that the report follows one meaningful thread across the year — but that every section reveals a different facet of that thread, at whichever scope actually serves it, landing an actual claim each time.

The goal is not maximal novelty, and it is not maximal caution. It's earned recurrence and earned confidence together: themes may return, phrasing should not; guidance may deepen, instructions should not loop; a report may be cohesive, it must never feel mass-produced; a claim may be humble about outcomes, it must never be hesitant about meaning.
