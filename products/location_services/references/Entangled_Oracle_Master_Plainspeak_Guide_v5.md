**Status:** V5.1. Supersedes and consolidates *Ultra-Plainspeak Astrological Translation Guide (V2)*, *plainspeak_translation_guide-v1.1*, *Ultra-Plainspeak Addendum (V3)*, and *Ultra-Plainspeak Translation Guide (V4)*. ## 0. What changed from V1–V4, and why

Before anything else, the corrections this version makes on purpose:

1. **"Never say the planet name" is retired as a rule.** It is now a *default posture*, not a prohibition. See §1.
2. **The old guides taught fill-in-the-bracket templates** (`"You are attracted to [specific qualities] in people"`). EO's real prose doesn't work that way — it's fully-written, specific, and already resolves the bracket before the reader ever sees it. This guide teaches the *reasoning* that produces a finished sentence, not a mad-lib.
3. **V2 and V4's marketing register — "Your Impact Radius," "you carry plot armor," "walking plot point," trademark-style Index™ names doing the grammatical work of a sentence — is not how EO actually talks.** Real EO copy (see §5) is literary, adult, and psychologically exact. It never sells the reader on how special the framework is; it just describes the reader accurately. Where old-guide phrasing was branding first and observation second, this version reverses that.
4. **V1–V4 only ever covered two layers**: the natal Big Three/planets/houses/aspects layer, and (in V3/V4) the proprietary asteroid-index layer. They said nothing about the layer that actually makes up most of a real EO report today: transits, progressions, solar arc, returns, profections, zodiacal releasing, eclipses, stations, ingresses, monthly snapshots, convergence windows, year integration. §4 covers that layer for the first time.
5. **None of the old guides addressed variation.** A translation guide that only tells you *what a Saturn transit means* will produce twelve technically-correct, emotionally identical paragraphs in a row. §6 is new: a bank of alternate openings, closings, registers, and guidance modes, organized by theme, so the same underlying astrology can be voiced a dozen different ways without repeating itself. This is the direct answer to "expand the inflection suggestions."
6. **The entity/archetype roster is updated and completed.** V3/V4 covered five proprietary indexes. The current engine ships those five plus a sixth (Dark Feminine Integration Score) and a much larger entity glossary (Sophia, Lilith BML, Lilith, Kaali, Circe, Medea, Hekate, Karma, Destinn, alongside the ones the old guides already had). §5.7 lists the full current roster.
7. **§7 no longer treats "avoid dogma" as a one-way instruction.** V5's first draft only corrected against rigidity. It's since been rebuilt around three separate axes — interpretive commitment, resonance scope, and determinism of objective fact — so relaxing "never say X" enforcement can't be misread as license to hedge every claim into vagueness, and "commit to a claim" can't be misread as license to predict specific future facts. See §7.

## 1. Design Philosophy: Translate for the Reader, Not Around the Machinery

The old design philosophies ("Hide the Machinery," "Translate the Architecture, Not the Planets") were written as absolutes: *never* say Sun, Moon, Rising, a planet, a house number, an aspect name, an asteroid. Treat that instinct as correct in spirit and wrong as a rule.

**The actual standard:** write for a reader who did not come here for a vocabulary lesson. Default to translating symbolic language into recognizable human behavior, because that's what makes the reader feel *seen* instead of *quizzed*. But this is a translation guide for content, not a dogma to enforce — the goal is always reader comprehension and felt accuracy, and there are real contexts where naming the mechanism is the more honest, more useful choice:

- **When the reader has asked "why did I get this."** Appendix-style, on-demand explanations (see §5.6, Suppression Logic) are exactly the place to say "Saturn," "12th house," "hard aspect." Hiding the mechanism there reads as evasive, not elegant.
- **When the placement name *is* the plainspeak.** "Scorpio Rising" is already a piece of natural language most readers who bought an astrology report understand; forcing it into "The Front Door" every time is often *more* jargon, not less, because now the reader has to translate the translation.
- **When two different mechanisms would otherwise produce identical-sounding copy.** If avoiding the word "Saturn" causes a station block and a transit block to collapse into the same sentence, name the mechanism instead of writing something generically true of everything.
- **In advanced/pro-tier surfaces** (a "show your work" toggle, a technical appendix, a synastry breakdown for a practicing astrologer-user) where the reader has explicitly opted into the technical layer.

So: **translate by default, name the mechanism when naming it serves the reader better than hiding it.** Every rule below that says "avoid X" means *this is the default lean*, not *this is forbidden*. Judgment beats the rule when they conflict.

## 2. The Real EO Sentence, Not the Bracket Template

The old guides taught structure like this:

> *"Your Fight & Drive (Mars): 'When crossed, you don't explode; you strategically dismantle the problem.'"*

That's a label plus a hypothetical example — useful as a brainstorming scaffold, but it's not a finished sentence a report can ship, and it invites the exact templated, mad-libs monotony the Anti-Monotony Protocol exists to prevent.

Compare it to how EO's actual Jupiter–Sun conjunction transit block resolves the same kind of "what does this mean for identity/confidence" territory:

> *"A sudden influx of scale and possibility lands directly on your sense of identity and personal vitality. This period asks you to inhabit more space in how you present yourself and what you believe you are capable of holding... The challenge is not to inflate your sense of self with temporary confidence, but to recognize what actually belongs to you now."*

Notice what's actually happening in real EO prose that the old guides never taught:

- **It resolves the bracket before the reader sees it.** There's no `[Specific Situation]` — the specific situation is already chosen and written.
- **It complicates itself in the same breath.** Nearly every EO block contains a "but" or a "the challenge is" — a second truth that keeps the reading from being a flat compliment or a flat warning. (This is the Anti-Monotony Protocol's "Complicate" function, §1.)
- **It trusts full sentences and subordinate clauses**, not declarative label-format ("Your X: Y"). The label-colon-example format reads as a spec sheet; EO reads as prose written by someone who has thought about the reader.
- **It never announces its own cleverness.** No trademarked score name is the grammatical subject of the sentence performing the insight. The insight does the work; the name of the system is metadata, not content.

**Working rule for any new or revised block:** if you can't remove the brackets and read it aloud as something a person would actually say to another person, it isn't finished. Write the resolved sentence, not the template.

## 3. Core Natal Layer (Big Three, Planets, Houses, Aspects, Patterns)

This section consolidates V1.1 and V2 — V1.1 for its structural completeness (orb-sensitivity, pattern coverage, the multi-part scaffold), V2 for its cleaner naming instinct — rewritten so the *output* matches §2's standard instead of the bracket-template standard.

### 3.1 The Big Three

Use evocative framing names as an occasional *organizing device* in summary/overview contexts (V2's instinct was right), but don't let the name replace the description, and don't force it into every sentence:

- **Sun — core identity, vitality, direction.** What a person is organizing their life around; where their sense of "being myself" comes from.
- **Moon — emotional processing, security needs, the autopilot.** What safety requires, how stress gets metabolized, what the reflexive (not chosen) response looks like.
- **Ascendant/Rising — approach, first contact, the immediate read a stranger gets.** Not "personality" — *strategy* for entering a new situation.

Translation principle, from real EO copy: name the *specific behavioral condition*, not the category. "You feel safe when your people are close" is doing real work; "you're an emotional person" is not. Every Big Three sentence should pass this test: could this sentence be true of the opposite sign too? If yes, it's still a category, not a translation.

### 3.2 Planets as Psychological Functions

Keep V1.1's functional framing (Mercury = processing/communication style, Venus = values/reciprocity, Mars = action/conflict style, etc.) — it's accurate and it holds up against the real engine's own natal-target logic (see the Anti-Monotony Protocol's Natal Target table, reproduced in §4.2). The correction is in the *execution*: don't stop at the label. EO's real natal-target logic distinguishes Sun (direction, authorship, visible purpose) from Mars (action style, stamina, desire) from Saturn (commitments, internal authority, durability) *by what specifically changes*, not by swapping in a different adjective for the same idea of "pressure" or "growth."

### 3.3 Houses as Life Arenas

Keep V2's plain-name convention (Your Wallet & Worth, Your Roots, Your Public Legacy, etc.) as a light label, but write the *content* the way EO writes it — as a described condition of life, not a keyword list:

1st — presence, physical approach, how you begin things
2nd — resources, self-worth, what you consider valuable
3rd — immediate environment, daily information, siblings/neighbors
4th — roots, home, emotional foundation
5th — creative self-expression, romance, play, what makes you feel alive

### 3.4 Aspects as Energetic Dynamics

Keep the plain-name conventions from V2 (Tag Team, Effortless Flow, Pressure Cooker, Tug-of-War) as light labels for reader-facing headers where a header is useful, but resolve the *actual mechanism* per the Anti-Monotony Protocol's Aspect Character logic — every aspect type should answer a different question, not a different flavor of the same question:

| Aspect | The actual editorial question |
|---|---|
| Conjunction | What is becoming concentrated, immediate, or newly inseparable? |
| Square | What mismatch is demanding movement, negotiation, or reconfiguration? |
| Opposition | What appears through contrast, another person, competing needs, or projection? |
| Trine | What can move with less resistance, and what still requires participation? |
| Sextile | What opening becomes real only through choice, response, or engagement? |
| Quincunx/Inconjunct | Where do two areas that don't naturally speak the same language require deliberate translation? |

V1.1's orb-sensitivity and modality-sensitivity (tight vs. loose conjunction; cardinal vs. fixed vs. mutable square) are worth keeping as a *depth* option for products that carry that data through — don't flatten every square into one sentence shape regardless of modality if the underlying calculation actually knows the difference.

### 3.5 Chart Patterns

Keep V1.1's full pattern set (Stellium, T-Square, Grand Trine, Yod) over V2's partial one — Yod matters and V2 dropped it. Translate each by its actual shape, not a generic "big deal" register:

- **Stellium** — a concentration of the personality in one theme; both the biggest gift and the biggest blind spot live in the same place.
- **T-Square** — a pressure triangle; the tension between two points gets discharged through a third (the apex), which becomes the pressure valve and the growth edge.
- **Grand Trine** — an easy, self-reinforcing loop between three points in one element; the risk is coasting on what's already easy rather than developing it.
- **Yod** — a specialized, oddly-shaped pressure aimed at one point from two others that don't easily relate to each other; it tends to produce a specific mission or talent rather than a general life theme.

## 4. The Forecast/Predictive Layer (new — not covered by any prior guide)

This is the layer that makes up most of an actual Year Ahead, Personal Forecast, or Soul Ecosystem report, and none of the four source guides addressed it. The **Anti-Monotony & Redundancy Protocol** already defines each layer's *job* precisely — this section folds that in as the layer-translation standard, since it was written against the real architecture and holds up.

### 4.1 Layer jobs (condensed from the Anti-Monotony Protocol)

| Layer | Job | Must avoid | Default resonance scope (§7.2) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Year Overview | Establish the year's broad climate and central developmental question | Listing every affected domain; giving the ending away early | Resonant-leaning — meant to feel widely recognizable before the report narrows |
| Archetypal Opening / EO Framing | Show how the reader's own constellation/index meets this year specifically | Paraphrasing the transit card underneath it in mystical language | Bridges resonant → revelatory |
| Monthly Snapshot | Name the month's particular rhythm before dated events | Predicting the cards below it; repeating last month's opening shape | Mixed — resonant atmosphere, revelatory selectivity |
| Transit Card | Interpret the specific symbolic contact | Generic planet boilerplate; universal "watch for overcommitment" | Revelatory-leaning — the specific contact is the whole point |
| Ingress | Mark a longer chapter through a whole-sign domain | Becoming a transit paragraph with a house name stapled on | Revelatory, at the scale of a life domain |
| Station | Describe a directional pause, review, or reorientation | "Retrograde = slow down," "direct = go" as a reflex | Revelatory |
| Convergence Window | Explain why events matter more together than apart | A poetic summary with no actual interaction effect | Revelatory — its justification is a synthesis no single card produces |
| Closing / Year Integration | Name what's changed by the report's own logic | Restating the Year Overview; the same closing line every time | Resonant-leaning, but earned by what preceded it |

### 4.2 Transit translation: four inputs, not one

A transit block is built from the *interaction* of four things — no single one is enough by itself:

**Transit planet + aspect character + natal target + natal house/domain.**

A Jupiter square to the Ascendant, a Jupiter square to Pluto, and a Jupiter opposition to Mars should never resolve to the same "growth, pressure, revision, boundaries" sentence with the nouns swapped. Use the planet-as-terrain table (§3.2) crossed with the aspect-as-question table (§3.4), landing on the *specific* natal target function, not a keyword.

### 4.3 Progressions, solar arc, and returns

These move slower than transits and mark *internal* development rather than external weather — translate the difference:

- **Progressions** — the chart's own internal maturation; what the person is *growing into*, largely independent of what's happening around them. Register: quieter, developmental, often invisible to others before it's visible to the reader.
- **Solar arc** — a compressed, symbolically "loud" version of the same internal arc; when it contacts something, it tends to mark a milestone-grade moment rather than ambient weather. Register: more decisive, more "this is the year that."
- **Returns** (Solar, Lunar, etc.) — a cyclical reset point; not a fresh start so much as a checkpoint where the whole cycle's business comes due for review. Register: retrospective-plus-directional — look back to see what this cycle was actually for, then set the frame for the one starting now.

### 4.4 Profections and zodiacal releasing (time-lord systems)

These name *which part of the chart is "in charge"* for a stretch of time — translate as a change of jurisdiction, not a mood:

- **Profections** — a specific house (and its ruling planet) takes the lead for the year; that planet's natal condition and house placement becomes the year's operating logic. Translate as "this year, the terrain that governs your experience is X's terrain" rather than a generic forecast wash.
- **Zodiacal releasing** — long-arc chapters (peak/growth/expansion vs. crisis/fallow "L2" periods) governing an entire life domain (career, fortune) for months or years at a time. Translate the *chapter type* honestly — a fallow/constricting period is not automatically bad, and a peak period is not automatically easy; each period has its own real texture, not a pass/fail grade.

### 4.5 Eclipses and stations

- **Eclipses** — accelerated, less negotiable turning points; something that was gradual becomes sudden. Register: significant without being catastrophic by default — avoid deterministic doom framing (see §7).
- **Stations** — see §4.1's station row. The specific planet stationing matters more than "retrograde" as a blanket concept; a Mercury station is not doing the same job as a Saturn station.

### 4.6 Ingresses, monthly snapshots, convergence, and integration

Already specified precisely in §4.1's table — the discipline here is almost entirely about *not* duplicating a neighboring layer's job. See §6.4 for the Convergence Integrity Test formula.

## 5. The Proprietary Index & Entity Layer

This section replaces V3 and V4 — the newest guide (V4) is the better foundation (its "translate the architecture, not the individual placement" instinct is correct for this layer specifically, since these indexes are already a synthesis of many placements). The corrections: drop the marketing register, keep the mechanism-hiding instinct only where the index is genuinely a synthesis, and complete the roster.

### 5.1 Core distinction this layer runs on: Archetype vs. Expression

- **Archetype** = the role (what function a person serves).
- **Expression** = the performance (how they execute that role, per their chart's specific modality/dominant entity).

Two people can share an archetype (both "Storytellers") and differ entirely in expression (one initiates the narrative, one preserves it). Keep this framing from V4 — it's sound and it matches how the engine actually works (a dominant-entity ArgMax determines archetype; the sign/placement of that entity determines expression).

### 5.2 The Catalyst Index — impact on others' timelines

Mechanism: Uranus/Chiron/Karma-Destinn contact to the descendant/relational axis. Translate as: what happens to *other people* because this person entered their life — not what this person feels, but what they cause.

- Uranus-dominant: sudden, unrequested change in the people this person meets.
- Chiron-dominant: an involuntary invitation to be more honest than is comfortable.
- Karma/Destinn-dominant: relationships that function like fated turning points for the other person, not just this one.

Write it as observation, not as a superlative ("you're a walking plot point" is the V3/V4 register to retire) — the real-EO equivalent register treats this as a described tendency with its own complication, the same way a transit block would.

### 5.3 The Reality Weaver Index — worldbuilding capacity

Mechanism: Arachne/Chaos-Hermes contact to the Midheaven/public-structure axis. Translate as the specific *strategy* of world-construction, not just "you're creative":

- Arachne-dominant: builds systems and communities other people move into and live inside, not just consume.
- Chaos/Hermes-dominant: dismantles rigid old structures to make room for new ones; the disruption is the constructive act, not a side effect.

### 5.4 The Kassandra Validation Quotient (KVQ)

Mechanism: hard aspects only (Mercury, Uranus, Saturn — easy aspects don't trigger this the same way), plus angularity and 12th-house placement. This is **not** "a strong intuition score" — it specifically measures the friction between *perceiving something correctly early* and *not being believed yet*. Being right isn't the point; the lived cost of being early is. Keep this distinction sharp — it's what makes KVQ different from a generic "you're intuitive" index, and it's already reflected accurately in the real engine's own docstring ("Being correct isn't enough; this score measures what happens when perception repeatedly collides with dismissal").

Real EO register for this (from the live identity_synthesis block, Epic genre / awakening state): *"The Kassandra current is teaching you to notice the early signal without treating every intuition as an emergency. What matters is learning which perception can be grounded in clear language, evidence, and timing."* — note this is doing real psychological work, not just validating the reader's ego. Match that register, not "you aren't crazy, you're just early" (V4's phrasing is closer to affirmation-poster than EO voice).

### 5.5 The Mythkeeper Index

Mechanism: Mnemosyne/Atlantis contact to the IC/nodal axis. Translate as a relationship to *preserved knowledge*, not "you like history":

- Mnemosyne-dominant (The Archivist): protects and preserves wisdom the world is at risk of forgetting.
- Atlantis-dominant (The Resurrector): an innate familiarity with lost systems; brings forgotten knowledge back to the surface.

This index answers "what story structure organizes your life," via four asteroid drivers (Apollo, Themis, Terpsichore, Sirene) each governing three genres, plus Aphrodite governing two:

### 5.7 Dark Feminine Integration Score (new — not in any prior guide)

Confirmed live in the current engine; none of V1–V4 mention it. Drivers include Lilith (Black Moon Lilith), Lilith (asteroid), Kaali, Circe, Medea, Hekate, Sophia. Translate this cluster as a relationship to disowned or culturally-punished forms of power — instinct, boundary, transformation through refusal, sovereignty that doesn't ask permission — rather than anything ominous. This is squarely an "avoid the generic warning" case (§7): the mechanism is about reclamation and integration, not danger, and copy should never default to a cautionary register just because the name sounds intense.

### 5.8 Suppression logic and activation states (keep from V4 — accurate and useful)

**Suppression / display thresholds:**
- 0.0 — hidden: not a primary driver, suppressed silently.
- 0.1–1.9 — subtle signal: present as background undercurrent.
- 2.0+ — display: a defining frequency, full index rendered.

**Activation states** (a relationship to the archetype, not a grade):
- **Awakening** — becoming aware of the pattern; it's calling but not yet fully lived.
- **Embodied** — actively living the pattern; operational, present-tense.
- **Sovereign** — the pattern operates without effort; it moves through the person rather than being managed by them.

Every index in §5.2–5.7 should be written with all three activation states available, matching the real engine's structure — not just a single fixed description per archetype.

## 6. Variation & Inflection — Writing the Same Truth Multiple Ways

This is the section none of the four old guides had, and it's the direct answer to "expand the inflection suggestions and ideas based on varying themes." A translation rule tells you *what* a placement means. It says nothing about *how many different ways* that meaning can be voiced — and without that, even perfectly accurate copy reads as templated the moment two blocks sit near each other in a real report. The Anti-Monotony Protocol diagnosed this problem precisely; what follows turns its rules into usable phrase banks organized by theme, so a writer has real alternatives on hand instead of just a list of things to avoid.

### 6.1 By emotional register (rotate — don't let every block land in the same one)

A full report needs range across: **steadiness, inquiry, permission, friction, recognition, experimentation, repair, enjoyment, privacy, visibility, commitment, release.** Concretely, that means not every block should be cautionary, therapeutic, solemn, or "wise" in the same register. Some blocks should simply grant permission, or notice something with pleasure, or sit in ambiguity without resolving it (§7.2).

| Register | What it sounds like |
|---|---|
| Steadiness | "This doesn't need to be dramatic to be real." |
| Inquiry | "The useful question here isn't X, it's Y." |
| Permission | "You're allowed to want this without earning it first." |
| Friction | "Two true things are pulling in different directions, and neither one is wrong." |
| Recognition | "You already know this about yourself — this just names it." |
| Experimentation | "Try the smaller version before you commit to the large one." |
| Repair | "This is the moment to say the thing you've been rehearsing." |
| Enjoyment | "Let this be as good as it actually feels." |
| Privacy | "Not everything here needs to be explained to anyone." |
| Visibility | "This is built to be seen — let it be seen." |
| Commitment | "This is the version of the choice that doesn't get walked back." |
| Release | "Some of this is simply finished." |

### 6.2 By opening move (never default to the same sentence shape twice in a row)

Instead of habitually opening with "This is a..." / "A [planet] current..." / "The focus is..." — rotate through:

- **A lived condition:** "The inbox is fuller than the calendar can absorb."
- **A contrast:** "What was effortless last spring now takes deliberate effort."
- **A question:** "What would this look like if it didn't require permission first?"
- **A concrete image:** "The door that's been half-open all year finally has weather coming through it."
- **A temporal shift:** "Something that has been building quietly reaches the surface."
- **An observed tension:** "Two commitments that used to fit easily now compete for the same hours."
- **A direct second-person invitation:** "Say the thing you've been editing down to something safer."
- **A statement of what becomes easier/harder to recognize:** "It gets harder to ignore what this relationship actually costs."

### 6.3 By closing move (never default to "choose what is sustainable")

Endings should do exactly one of three things — sharpen a question, offer a precise next orientation, or leave a clean image, not restate the caution:

- *Sharpen:* "The real question isn't whether to say yes — it's whether you'd still say yes without the audience."
- *Orient:* "Start with the version of this that doesn't require anyone else's approval yet."
- *Image:* "The room is finally big enough to hold what you actually want to put in it."

### 6.4 By guidance mode (rotate the *kind* of practical support, not just the wording)

Not every "challenging" block needs a boundary, and not every "flowing" block needs restraint. Available modes: **boundary, pacing, evidence, conversation, structure, experiment, embodiment, discernment, receiving, release, visibility, protection, meaning-making.** Match the mode to the actual mechanism (a Neptune contact rarely calls for "structure"; a Saturn contact rarely calls for "just feel it out").

### 6.5 By metaphor family (limit saturation, rotate the system)

EO already leans on pressure/expansion/thresholds/containers/currents language heavily enough that it needs deliberate counterweight. Rotate in: **weather** (conditions, visibility, timing), **architecture** (supports, repair, access), **music** (rhythm, dissonance, pause), **craft** (iteration, calibration), **ecology** (interdependence, succession), **navigation** (orientation, course correction), **conversation** (terms, listening, negotiation), **theatre** (role, rehearsal, audience), **domestic life** (routines, agreements, belonging). A metaphor only earns its place if it clarifies the actual mechanism — not decoration for its own sake.

### 6.6 By life-domain specificity (don't let five blocks all gesture at "identity")

If several blocks in the same report all touch identity/confidence/public presence, each one needs to name a *different specific condition* within that domain rather than cycling the same three nouns: what's changing about *how* identity is expressed, not just that "identity" is the theme again.

### 6.7 The practical test before shipping a block

Borrowed directly from the Anti-Monotony Protocol because it's the sharpest tool in any of the source material: **"After reading the previous block, what does this block let the reader understand, notice, or do that they couldn't already?"** If the honest answer is "it says the same thing more poetically" or "it's technically about a different transit," revise it. And the corollary: **"What exact sentence would become less true if this block were removed?"** If the report would say basically the same thing without it, it isn't earning its place.

## 7. Certainty, Resonance, and Determinism: Three Different Axes

The first draft of this section made one correction — relax rigid, over-enforced dogma. That correction is still right, but stated alone it's dangerous: read in isolation, it can just as easily excuse a block that hedges into fog as one that overreaches into false certainty. What follows separates three axes that are easy to collapse into one another, but govern completely different things. Keep them separate — a rule that's correct on one axis is often exactly backwards if applied to another.

### 7.1 Axis One: Interpretive Commitment (hedged vs. landed)

Every instruction in §1–§6 that reads like an absolute (avoid X, don't say Y) is a strong default, not a compliance rule to mechanically enforce. Treat deviations as judgment calls, not violations. That's the "relax the dogma" correction, and it still holds.

But it is not permission to hedge. A block can be gentle, ethical, and beautifully written, and still fail — if it refuses to make a clear interpretive move. Softness and ambiguity can be used to avoid naming the actual pattern, and that's a defect even though it can look, at a glance, like appropriate humility. Watch for: every claim cushioned with "may/might/could/invites"; a paragraph that circles a theme without landing anywhere; language that sounds wise but never says what's distinct about *this* configuration; mood offered in place of mechanism; a closing sentence that retreats into a generic reflection question; prose that avoids being wrong by becoming too vague to be useful.

The fix is not to become absolute. The fix is to become specific.

| Instead of | Try |
|---|---|
| "This may invite you to consider where a pattern around visibility could be asking for more care." | "Visibility is not the problem here. The strain comes from being seen before the structure behind your role has caught up." |
| "You may be asked to reflect on whether an old form of belonging still supports who you are becoming." | "A familiar form of belonging can start to feel too narrow now, not because it was false, but because it was built around an earlier version of your needs." |

A concrete, checkable version of this rule: **hedge once, don't stack.** One softening qualifier per claim is fine and often necessary. Three stacked on one clause ("it might possibly suggest there could potentially be some indication") reads as an author who doesn't trust their own observation — sharpen the claim rather than adding a second qualifier.

Also on this axis: don't force every block into a wound→wisdom or pressure→empowerment arc. Real life includes ambiguity, ordinary maintenance, pleasure, quiet recognition, temporary uncertainty, rest without moral framing, and receiving without having earned it first. Not every symbolic contact requires a breakthrough — and an unbroken healing-arc structure quietly implies the reader is always mid-lesson, never just living, which is its own kind of overclaiming.

**Where this axis stops:** a block can be maximally landed and unhedged and still say nothing false, because everything on this axis is about *how confidently an interpretive pattern is described* — not about *what kind of claim* is being made. That's §7.3.

### 7.2 Axis Two: Resonance Scope (broad/Barnum vs. narrow/revelatory) — a business choice, not a virtue

A separate axis from 7.1, easy to confuse with it. This one is about how broadly a claim is meant to apply, not how confidently it's stated.

- **Resonant (Barnum-shaped):** broad statements a large number of readers will recognize in themselves. This is normal cognition — the same mechanism underneath poetry, good therapy, and any writing that makes someone feel seen — not a psychological vulnerability to be exploited carefully or described with suspicion. It does real work: it's what makes a Year Overview or a monthly theme feel immediately recognizable, and drives the "this really gets me" reaction that brings a reader back.
- **Revelatory (explicit):** narrow, individuated claims that follow from *this specific chart's* computed placements and would read as false or generic for most other readers. This is what justifies the depth of a premium/deep-report tier — the reader is paying for *their* chart, not a mood.

Neither is more honest than the other, and neither is a fallback default. The actual failure mode is using vague, uncommitted language (§7.1's problem) as a way to dodge picking either one. A resonant statement can be fully landed and unhedged ("You feel everything more than people give you credit for"). A revelatory statement can be fully landed and unhedged ("The strain isn't the promotion — it's that the systems around you haven't caught up to it yet"). Both are correct uses of confident prose; a statement so hedged it fails at being either is the only wrong answer.

Scope should default by layer (see the table in §4.1, now with a scope column) — Year Overview and Closing/Integration lean resonant by design; Transit Card, Ingress, and Convergence lean revelatory by design; Monthly Snapshot and the EO Framing layer bridge the two. This is currently editorial discipline (a writer has to know and apply it); a future block-system pass is expected to make resonance scope a structural property blocks carry rather than something held only in a writer's head.

### 7.3 Axis Three: The Determinism Boundary (objective fact vs. interpretive pattern) — read this one on its own terms

This is not a certainty-of-tone question, and it should never be resolved by turning up or down how confidently something is phrased. It's a different category of claim.

*"This transit favors visibility becoming harder to avoid"* is an interpretive claim about a pattern — landed, unhedged, entirely appropriate at any confidence level. *"You will marry the person you met at 12, on October 17, 2028, and move 3.17 miles away"* is not a more-committed version of that same sentence. It's a falsifiable claim about objective future fact, presented as guidance rather than as the symbolic material it actually is — and no amount of softening or firming the phrasing moves it across that line, because the line isn't about phrasing.

Why this matters beyond craft: treating a report as a definitive, objective source of cosmic guidance is a legal exposure, and past a point an ethical one — if a reader without solid footing restructures real decisions and stability around a report because it was written to sound like fact rather than symbolic material, that's a duty-of-care failure, not a writing-quality one.

Explicit deterministic prediction has a home: the Predictive Sandbox lane, deliberately contained there as experimentation — not in Year Ahead, Personal Forecast, Soul Ecosystem, or Identity Profile, regardless of how §7.1 and §7.2 resolve for those products.

If you're using the Observatory Team review roles: Sentinel's actual trigger is this axis — an objective-fact claim dressed as guidance — not a confidently-written interpretive sentence. A review that invokes Sentinel to walk back a block that correctly followed §7.1's no-hedging standard has confused the two axes; the review is wrong, not the block.

## 8. Quick Editorial Checklist

Before shipping or revising a block:

- [ ] Does it read as a finished sentence, not a bracket template? (§2)
- [ ] Does it name the *specific* mechanism (which planet, which aspect, which natal target) rather than a swappable generic? (§3–4)
- [ ] If it's an index/entity block, does it match the real engine's roster and activation-state structure, not an outdated name? (§5)
- [ ] Does it differ from its likely neighbors in at least two of: register, opening shape, closing shape, metaphor family, guidance mode? (§6)
- [ ] Would the report say something different if this block were removed? (§6.7)
- [ ] Does it avoid a generic caution or a forced healing arc where the mechanism doesn't call for one? (§7.1)
- [ ] Does it land at least one clear interpretive claim rather than hedge-stacking into fog? (§7.1)
- [ ] Is its resonance scope (broad/resonant vs. narrow/revelatory) appropriate to its layer's job? (§7.2)
- [ ] Does it stay clear of the determinism line — an interpretive pattern, not an objective-fact prediction? (§7.3)
- [ ] Is any jargon-avoidance a deliberate choice for *this* context, not a reflex? (§1)
