## Purpose

Specify how signals from multiple method families compose into `ChapterState` and `MicroCandidate` objects; how anti-double-counting is enforced across families; and the discrete-event candidate protocol, including the trigger-derived window rule that replaces any fixed universal maximum.

**Correction note (2026-07-07):** an earlier draft of this program stated a fixed "six calendar day" maximum candidate window as a general architectural rule. That number was only ever intended as a `predictive_sandbox` research-protocol constant (an internal stress-testing parameter), not a rule for the general system. It has been corrected here: outside the sandbox, a candidate's window is derived from the natural boundaries of the trigger event(s) that anchor it, with no artificial ceiling. See §4.2.

## 1. Candidate classes and their rules

Four output classes. The class is a hard type distinction, not a scoring band.

### 1.1 Weather

Broad structural activity.

- Composed from: sustained transits (Saturn/Uranus/Neptune/Pluto cycles), retrograde clusters, void-of-course clusters, ZR L1/L2 periods, profection year, long-duration progressions.
- **Not** an output object per se. Weather is expressed as the *daily_series* baseline field, structural signal lists, and active `TimeLordPeriod` records.
- **Never** counted as a discrete-event hit or as a candidate. Weather cannot be a `MicroCandidate`.
- Report-surface visibility: internal + sandbox. Under the 2026-07-08 Phase 9b operator decision, the Year Ahead climate section may inherit weather signals when framed as predictive/experimental content.

### 1.2 Chapter (`ChapterState`)

A bounded but potentially long arc with strong natal / topic relevance.

Requires:

- ≥1 long-clock source (return, progression, Solar Arc, profection, ZR, or a sustained transit whose duration > 90 days);
- ≥1 natal-promise anchor link;
- topic coherence across contributing signals (see §3);
- retained start/end basis (peak_range non-empty).

Emission rule: chapters are generated by the chapter builder that runs *after* signal normalization and *before* candidate assembly.

Report-surface visibility: internal + sandbox at Phase 6+. Under the 2026-07-08 Phase 9b operator decision, Year Ahead and Personal Forecast may surface ChapterState modules when framed as predictive/experimental content.

### 1.3 Trigger

An exact or short-lived event.

Sources: transit exactness, station, ingress, plain lunation, eclipse, return moment, solar-arc-exact contact, progression-exact contact, ZR L1/L2/L3/L4 transition, profection year handoff, direct asteroid contact.

- **Not** an output object separate from `ForecastEvent` / `PredictiveSignal`. A trigger is a signal with `signal_role = "trigger_evidence"`.
- **Never** a discrete-event candidate on its own. A trigger contributes to a candidate but does not constitute one.

### 1.4 Discrete Candidate (`MicroCandidate`)

The narrow prospectively-generated interval. **No fixed maximum window.** The window is derived from the natural boundaries of the anchoring trigger signal(s) — see §4.2. `predictive_sandbox` may separately enforce its own internal research cap (currently 6 calendar days) as a stress-testing constraint; that cap is sandbox-scoped and does not apply to the general system. All rules in §4.

## 2. Anti-stacking policy

The core rule from the program: *a method family gets one independent vote.* Implementation:

### 2.1 Independence groups

Every `ForecastEvent` and `PredictiveSignal` carries an `independence_group` (see [01_predictive_object_schemas.md](./01_predictive_object_schemas.md) §9). Independence groups are the atomic units of vote counting.

### 2.2 Composite deduplication

For `MicroCandidate.independent_method_families`, the candidate builder counts distinct entries after deduplicating on the composite key `(independence_group, source_body, target_body)`. This handles the edge case where a transit and a proprietary asteroid transit involving the same natal target should collapse.

Specifically:

- `transit_family` and `proprietary_transit_family` collapse when the same `(source_body, target_body)` pair is involved. Example: transiting Uranus square natal Sun via `scan_transit_windows` and transiting Uranus square natal Sun via `scan_proprietary_forecast_windows` count once.
- `transit_family` and `proprietary_transit_family` **do not** collapse when the source or target differs. Example: transiting Uranus square natal Sun (transit_family) and transiting Chaos conjunct natal Kassandra (proprietary_transit_family) count independently.

### 2.3 Transit sub-type collapse

Within `transit_family` (and its proprietary variant), the following sub-types describe *one* underlying cycle and collapse into one vote:

- transit cycle (`method_variant = transit_cycle`);
- station of the same body on that natal target within the cycle window (`method_variant = station`);
- retrograde-pass phases of that cycle (`phase = first_pass / retrograde_pass / third_pass`);
- house ingress of the same body during that cycle window (`method_variant = ingress`).

Rule: signals sharing `(cycle_id, source_body, target_body)` collapse to one vote in candidate assembly.

### 2.4 Lunation and eclipse

- Eclipse suppresses same-date plain lunation upstream (already implemented in `scan_lunations` via `eclipse_dates` set).
- At candidate assembly, an eclipse and a plain lunation on adjacent months count as one `lunation_family` vote when they both target the same natal target *and* fall within 45 days of the candidate peak. Otherwise they count independently within the same family — which is still one vote total, per the family's independence rule.

### 2.5 Return + transit of returned body

A solar return and a transit of transiting Sun during the same window (e.g. solar return day + transiting Sun conjunct natal Sun) are the same underlying phenomenon. They collapse per the rule in [03_method_charters.md](./03_method_charters.md) §C1.8.

### 2.6 Progression + return

Progressed Sun conjunct natal Sun during the same window as a solar return: same underlying anchor (natal Sun highlighted at this age). Collapse per [03_method_charters.md](./03_method_charters.md) §C3.8.

### 2.7 Solar Arc + Progression

Solar Arc and secondary progressions are counted as independent families even when they target the same natal body. Justification in [03_method_charters.md](./03_method_charters.md) §C2.8 and §C3.8: the calculations are genuinely distinct.

### 2.8 Time-lord families

- Fortune ZR and Spirit ZR: independent (`zr_family_fortune` vs `zr_family_spirit`). Two votes when both are active.
- Profection: `profection_family` — one vote per candidate window.
- Time-lord + transit of the current time lord: independent if the transit is not itself the time-lord activation. In practice they are almost always independent because the time-lord identity is a static year property, and the transit is a dynamic exactness event.

### 2.9 Asteroid stacking

Multiple asteroid contacts (e.g. transiting Uranus conjunct natal Kassandra + transiting Uranus square natal Aletheia) contribute to `asteroid_specificity` and `topic_coherence` component scores but do **not** inflate `independent_method_families` count on their own. They belong to the same `transit_family` (or `proprietary_transit_family`) vote.

## 3. Topic coherence

Convergence requires topic coherence — evidence pointing at the same natal-promise focus. Coherence is checked at chapter and candidate assembly, not at signal emission.

### 3.1 Coherence graph

Two signals are topic-coherent if any of the following are true:

1. They activate at least one shared `natal_anchor_id`.
2. Their target bodies share ≥1 house.
3. Their `topic_keys` sets have Jaccard overlap ≥ 0.34 (at least one shared topic out of 3-ish typical topic keys per signal).
4. Their `domain_keys` sets have any overlap.
5. Their target bodies participate in the same proprietary index driver set.
6. They point at bodies connected by a natal aspect matrix cell with orb ≤ 3°.
7. They point at bodies in the same rulership chain (target A is ruled by body X; target B is body X or is ruled by X).

The coherence graph on a candidate window is: nodes are contributing signals; edges are any of the seven rules above.

### 3.2 Chapter coherence

A `ChapterState` requires: the coherence graph restricted to its contributing signals is a single connected component, OR every non-connected component has ≥2 signals of its own (so a chapter can have a primary and a secondary strand but not disconnected singletons).

`ChapterState.coherence` numerical value in `[0, 1]`: `edges_present / edges_possible` where `edges_possible = n * (n-1) / 2` and `n = |contributing_signals|`.

### 3.3 Candidate coherence

A `MicroCandidate` requires: the coherence graph restricted to the *anchor + chapter_support + trigger_support* signals is a single connected component. No disconnected strands are permitted in a discrete candidate.

`MicroCandidate.component_scores["topic_coherence"]` in `[0, 1]` computed the same way.

## 4. Discrete candidate protocol

### 4.1 Emission requirements (hard)

All of the following must be true at emission time. If any fails, no candidate is emitted for that peak.

1. `window_days` is fully derived from trigger boundaries per §4.2 — no fixed ceiling to satisfy, but the window must be non-empty (`window_days > 0`) and must not exceed the natural span of its own qualifying trigger evidence (see §4.2's "never expand" rule).
2. `|natal_promise_links| ≥ 1`.
3. `|chapter_support| ≥ 1` — must include at least one long-clock signal OR one sustained-transit signal with duration > 90 days.
4. `|trigger_support| ≥ 1` — must include at least one signal with `signal_role = "trigger_evidence"` and `temporal_precision ∈ {"instant", "day"}`.
5. `|independent_method_families| ≥ 2` — after composite deduplication per §2.2.
6. Coherence: single connected component per §3.3.
7. No unresolved anti-double-counting violation (validated by the candidate builder against §2).
8. Confidence: every contributing signal has `confidence_state ≠ "withheld"` OR the candidate carries `birth_time_dependency = "hard"` and its own `candidate_status = "withheld"`.
9. `alternative_evidence` retained: at least one non-qualifying near-miss signal from the same window must be listed in `alternative_evidence` if any exist in the daily-series scan. This is a positive requirement, not just "if convenient" — the builder must attempt to collect near-misses.
10. `pre_registered_at` set once at emission; sidecar written before any outcome review.

### 4.2 Window construction

- Only signals with `signal_role = "trigger_evidence"` AND `temporal_precision ∈ {"instant", "day"}` may anchor a `MicroCandidate` (per emission requirement §4.1.4). This constraint is what keeps candidate windows narrow — it is a qualification rule on the evidence, not a cap on the output.
- The candidate window is the union of the natural `[start_at, end_at]` span of every qualifying trigger signal included in the candidate. A station's window is its own single date. An eclipse's or lunation's window is its own narrow orb window. An exact transit contact's window is its own entry/exit at day-precision. A return moment's window is its own small buffer (per the relevant method charter in [03_method_charters.md](./03_method_charters.md)).
- `peak_at` is the peak of the highest-strength qualifying trigger signal within the window.
- **Never manufacture width.** If the natural trigger window is one day, the candidate window is one day. There is no minimum to pad toward and no maximum to expand toward.
- If two or more trigger signals contribute distinct peaks that are topic-coherent (share ≥1 anchor, per §3.1) and whose natural windows are adjacent or overlapping (gap ≤ 1 day between one trigger's `end_at` and the next trigger's `start_at`), they assemble into one candidate spanning their combined natural range. If they are not topic-coherent, or their natural windows are not adjacent, they assemble into separate candidates even if their peaks happen to fall in the same week.
- In practice, because qualifying triggers are day/instant-precision by construction, resulting candidate windows are typically narrow (single-digit days) without needing an imposed ceiling. If a future validation cycle finds this produces windows wider than the program intends, that is a signal to revisit the `temporal_precision` qualification rule in §4.1.4 — not to reintroduce a blanket cap.

### 4.3 No post-hoc shrinking

`window_days` is fixed at emission. If a later outcome-review process notices an event that fell just outside the window, the outcome is coded as `unresolved` per the validation protocol; the candidate is not retroactively narrowed.

If the *candidate builder* itself detects a calculation error after emission (e.g. a wrong return moment), the candidate is `voided` with a documented reason. A `voided` candidate does not become a "hit" for a different date range.

### 4.4 Candidate ID stability

`candidate_id = "cand_" + hash(chart_snapshot_id, policy_version, peak_at, trigger_signal_ids sorted)`. Regenerating the same report against the same policy version produces the same candidate ID. Changing the policy produces a new candidate (with a new ID); the old candidate remains in the record.

### 4.5 Multiple candidates per report

A report window may produce multiple candidates. There is no maximum number. Ranking is by `convergence_score`, but every candidate meeting the emission requirements is retained.

### 4.6 Zero candidates is a valid outcome

If no peak in the report window meets the emission requirements, the sidecar records zero candidates and lists all near-miss peaks in the rejected-candidate registry. This is the "quiet period" case and it must be preserved for the validation program.

## 5. Component score preservation

`MicroCandidate.component_scores` retains named contributors so ranking is transparent.

### 5.1 Required component names

- `natal_promise_strength` — max strength of contributing anchors, weighted by anchor confidence.
- `long_clock_support` — max signal_strength among chapter_support signals.
- `trigger_precision` — inverse of `min(orb_of_trigger_signals)`; higher for tighter contact.
- `method_family_diversity` — count of distinct `independent_method_families` after deduplication.
- `topic_coherence` — value from §3.3.
- `temporal_convergence` — how close the trigger peaks cluster within the window; higher for tighter clustering.
- `asteroid_specificity` — 0 if no asteroid participants; higher for asteroid participants with tighter orb.
- `confidence` — geometric mean of contributing signal confidences.
- `counterforce` — see §6.
- `complexity` — number of active operation axes across contributing signals.
- `window_width` — `window_days` normalized to `[0, 1]` via a documented decay scale (narrower is higher): `window_width = exp(-window_days / reference_scale_days)`, where `reference_scale_days` is a calibration constant (Phase 0 default `3.0`, tuned during Phase 9 validation) — a normalization reference, not a hard ceiling on candidate emission.

### 5.2 Composite convergence score

`convergence_score` is a weighted mean of the components. Weights are versioned and stored in the sidecar's `policy_version`. Suggested Phase 0 defaults (subject to validation calibration):

```
natal_promise_strength   0.14
long_clock_support       0.14
trigger_precision        0.14
method_family_diversity  0.12
topic_coherence          0.12
temporal_convergence     0.08
asteroid_specificity     0.06
confidence               0.10
counterforce            -0.05
complexity               0.02
window_width             0.03
```

Component weights are documented, not opaque. Ranking uses `convergence_score`; components remain individually visible for review.

## 6. Counterforce handling

A candidate may have genuine internal conflict without being void.

### 6.1 What counts as counterforce

- Signals with opposed dominant operations inside the coherence graph (e.g. one signal dominant `activate`, another dominant `stabilize`, both aimed at the same anchor).
- Signals with opposing valence at the anchor level: an anchor's proprietary index driver is transited constructively while a related anchor's driver is transited disruptively.
- A trigger contradicting a chapter's directionality (e.g. Saturn chapter of `stabilize` interrupted by a lunation with dominant `disrupt`).

### 6.2 What is *not* counterforce

- Multiple structurally consistent triggers within a candidate. Redundant reinforcement is not conflict.
- A trigger unrelated to any anchor. That is filtered as noise, not treated as counterforce.
- Complexity across operation axes. Complexity is its own component.

### 6.3 Counterforce score

`counterforce ∈ [0, 1]`. Computed as: sum over contributing signals of `max(0, opposition_to_dominant_direction)`, normalized by contributing signal count.

### 6.4 Counterforce and validation

Candidates with `counterforce ≥ 0.5` are still emitted but flagged `counterforce_high = true`. They participate in validation like any other candidate. The validation protocol tracks whether high-counterforce candidates hit or miss at different rates.

## 7. Alternative evidence retention

Every candidate retains signals and events that participated in its window's decision-making but did not enter the qualifying set. This is `alternative_evidence`.

### 7.1 What must be retained

- Near-miss signals: signals with `signal_strength ≥ 0.5 * max_contributing_signal_strength` that were not selected due to coherence-graph disconnection.
- Rejected trigger candidates: peaks in the daily series within the same window whose prominence was below threshold or whose method-family did not fit the composite dedup.
- Suppressed anti-stacked signals: the second-and-later signals of a collapsed group (only the "winner" enters `chapter_support` or `trigger_support`; the others go here).

### 7.2 Format

`alternative_evidence` is a list of `signal_id` OR `event_id` values. The sidecar preserves the full signal / event objects; the candidate only stores the references.

## 8. Chapter builder ↔ candidate builder handoff

Chapters are built first. Candidates reference chapters via `chapter_support`, not via direct signal references, for long-clock support.

**Phase 7 overall build order (not previously stated explicitly — this is the actual dependency chain, not a suggestion):**

1. Build the `NatalPromiseAnchor` set for the chart (`phase0/01_predictive_object_schemas.md` §1).
2. Run the anchor-matcher over every existing `ForecastEvent` and `PredictiveSignal`, populating `natal_anchor_ids` — retroactively for events already produced by the six existing method families, not only prospectively for new ones.
3. Run the chapter builder (§8.1 below) — this step depends on step 2's populated `natal_anchor_ids`, since chapter clustering keys on shared anchor.
4. Run cross-clock convergence / candidate assembly (Phase 8) — depends on step 3's `ChapterState` objects existing.

Building the chapter builder or convergence scoring before anchors exist will produce empty or nonsensical clusters; there is no way to do this out of order.

### 8.1 Chapter builder order

1. Aggregate active `TimeLordPeriod` records into their durations. **Granularity rule (not previously stated): one `ChapterState` per top-level (root) period occurrence — one per annual profection year, one per Zodiacal Releasing L1 occurrence — not one per period record at every level.** Child-level `TimeLordPeriod` records (ZR L2/L3/L4 sitting under an L1, or a monthly profection sitting under an annual one, if ever added) contribute to their parent chapter's `contributing_signal_ids`/`active_long_clocks` and may adjust its `weight_modifier`, but do not themselves become separate `ChapterState` objects. This keeps `chapter_kind` values (`profection_year`, `zr_period`, etc.) meaningful as single objects per real-world period, not fragmented across four levels.
2. Identify sustained transit cycles with `duration_days > 90`, computed from each signal's own `start_date`/`end_date` (`phase0/01_predictive_object_schemas.md` §3) — no new duration field to invent.
3. Cluster signals by shared anchor + shared time window. **"Shared time window" (not previously quantified): a signal qualifies for a chapter's cluster when its `[start_date, end_date]` range overlaps the chapter's own range, or falls within 30 days of it.** This is deliberately wider than the candidate builder's ±3-day discovery buffer (§8.2) because chapters operate at month/year scale, not day scale — reusing the candidate builder's tighter buffer here would silently exclude most of the long-clock signals a chapter is supposed to gather.
4. Emit `ChapterState` per cluster meeting the chapter rules (§1.2). **`ChapterState.confidence`, `counterforce`, and `complexity` reuse the same formulas as their `MicroCandidate` equivalents (§5.1's confidence formula, §6.3's counterforce formula, and the complexity definition in §5.1), applied to the chapter's own `contributing_signal_ids` set — do not derive a separate formula for chapters.** `ChapterState.coherence` already has its own explicit formula (§3.2); this note covers the three fields that didn't.

### 8.2 Candidate builder order

1. Iterate over trigger-signal peaks in the report window, restricted to signals with `temporal_precision ∈ {"instant", "day"}`.
2. For each peak, scan a small operational discovery buffer (±3 days, for candidate-clustering discovery only — not a width constraint) for other coherent trigger signals to potentially merge per §4.2's adjacency rule.
3. Match anchors from natal promise graph.
4. Match chapter support from emitted `ChapterState` objects and long-clock signals.
5. Match trigger support from trigger-role signals.
6. Compute component scores.
7. Check hard requirements (§4.1); if any fails, log to rejected-candidate registry with `filter_reason`.
8. If passing, emit `MicroCandidate`.

### 8.3 Rejected-candidate registry

Every peak examined that did not become a candidate is recorded with:

- peak date;
- residual;
- prominence;
- what was missing (which of the 10 hard requirements failed);
- what evidence was present but insufficient;
- method families that were considered;
- signal IDs that participated in the decision.

Format specified in [06_sidecar_and_export_contract.md](./06_sidecar_and_export_contract.md).

## 9. Idempotency and reproducibility

- Given the same natal snapshot, same policy version, same asteroid registry version, and same report window, the same candidates must be emitted with the same IDs.
- Any downstream change (asteroid registry bump, method charter revision, weight tuning) is a new policy version. Old sidecars remain valid records of what the system said at the time.

## 10. Report-surface routing (candidate class)

**Operator decision, 2026-07-08 — the "Phase 10 governance" gate below is
lifted, folded into Phase 9b (see
`EO_UPGRADE_PHASES_1_9_AGENT_PROMPTS.md`).** Phase 9 is the last
actively-scoped phase in this program; the operator chose not to run a
separate Phase 10 effort and instead authorized report-facing promotion
now, framed as predictive/experimental content, validated alongside via
the Phase 9 outcome ledger rather than gated on it.

- `Weather` → internal + sandbox always; Year Ahead climate section may now surface per Phase 9b, framed as predictive/experimental.
- `ChapterState` → internal + sandbox always; Year Ahead chapter modules and Personal Forecast may now surface per Phase 9b, framed as predictive/experimental.
- `Trigger` (as signal) → internal + sandbox always; not directly surfaced in Phase 9b (chapters and candidates are the report-facing units, not raw signals).
- `MicroCandidate` → internal + validation lab always; **may now also surface in Personal Forecast per Phase 9b**, explicitly hedged as a research-flagged window, never presented as a settled prediction.

This updates the program rule "No new clock enters report prose before it enters the evidence sidecar and validation harness" to its Phase 9b form: report prose now may draw directly from the evidence sidecar's own objects (chapters, candidates), with the validation harness running in parallel rather than as a precondition. The candidate/chapter objects remain the same objects computed for the sidecar — report prose is an additional consumer, not a separate pipeline.

## 11. Locked at Phase 0

- Trigger-derived candidate window; no fixed universal maximum. `predictive_sandbox` retains its own separate internal research cap (currently 6 calendar days), scoped to that surface only.
- Four candidate classes; each is a type, not a score band.
- Composite dedup rules (§2.2).
- Ten hard requirements for `MicroCandidate` emission (§4.1).
- No post-hoc shrinking (§4.3).
- Alternative evidence retention (§7).
- Rejected-candidate registry (§8.3).
- Component score preservation (§5).
- Phase 7 build order: anchors → anchor-matching → chapter builder → convergence/candidates (§8).
- One `ChapterState` per top-level `TimeLordPeriod` occurrence, not per level (§8.1).

Any change to these requires a new policy version and an operator note in `agents/REVISIONS.md`.
